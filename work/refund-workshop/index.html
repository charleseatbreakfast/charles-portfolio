Starting point

Refund feedback pointed to a service journey problem.

July customer feedback placed Return & Refund satisfaction at 61, below the Customer Service score of 71. The goal was to improve the refund experience, reduce return and refund issues, and work toward a target satisfaction score of 70.

The experience extended beyond the app. Customer Service operations, communication, collection arrangements, finance approvals and banking processes all shaped what customers saw and how long each step took.

Mapping the existing journey

The journey map revealed three connected problem areas.

The team mapped four stages from receiving the package to receiving the refund. Grouping the observations exposed recurring problems across channels, handoffs and status communication.

  1. 01

    Discoverability & clarity

    Customers struggled to locate refund actions and policies across fragmented touchpoints. The generic “Enquiry” entry point did not clearly signal after-sales support.

  2. 02

    Communication & manual handoffs

    Customers moved between the app, email, hotline and other channels. Refund preferences and collection arrangements required repeated exchanges without a clear response time.

  3. 03

    Refund visibility & transparency

    Existing statuses did not explain actual refund progress. Customers lacked clear information about completion timing, refund method, collection status and voucher details.

Role-play & service flow refinement

From role-play observations to service flow refinement.

The team used role-play to examine how the proposed customer experience could operate alongside the existing Customer Service process.

I observed the customer and Customer Service simulation, documented issues and discussion findings, and helped refine the proposed workflow based on the team’s observations.

This connected customer-facing pain points with operational requirements around communication, information collection and refund status visibility. The comparisons below consolidate documented outputs from across the workshop; they are not presented as role-play findings alone.

01

Reducing manual communication.

Existing
Customer Service manually edited email templates to request refund preferences and coordinate returns.
Proposed
Use structured digital forms and automated email or app notifications to collect and confirm relevant details.
Design rationale
Reduce reliance on repeated manual exchanges and give customers and Customer Service a more structured process.
02

Improving refund status visibility.

Existing
Internal statuses such as “已處理” and “已接納申請” did not explain the customer’s actual refund progress.
Proposed
Add explicit refund and collection statuses, supported by notifications at relevant stages.
Design rationale
Help customers understand progress and next steps without repeatedly contacting Customer Service.
03

Identifying operational dependencies.

Dependencies
Collection time slots from Delivery, integration with SAP, TOS and eRefund, Finance approval requirements, and visibility into successful bank refunds.
Design rationale
Make operational and technical dependencies explicit before implementation decisions are made.

Workshop-voted priorities

Five directions participants considered most valuable.

Participant voting recorded workshop preferences for further discussion. These ideas were not technically validated, approved for implementation or confirmed as delivery commitments.

  1. 7

    Replace email exchanges with an online form for preferred delivery timing and refund method.

  2. 7

    Reorganise Order History and refund status information.

  3. 6

    Consolidate Customer Service contact channels.

  4. 5

    Rename “Enquiry” to “After Sales Follow-Up”.

  5. 5

    Add refund and collection status notifications.

Preliminary follow-up structure

A three-sprint proposal organised the ideas for further review.

The report grouped possible UX enhancements into three sprints. Technical feasibility, business impact, Customer Service implications and cross-functional dependencies still required review before implementation planning.

Outcome & reflection

Improving the refund experience required the team to examine the whole service behind each screen.

The workshop brought customer-facing, design, business and technical perspectives into one view of refund journey friction. The team refined a proposed Customer Service workflow, identified operational dependencies and created a clearer basis for feasibility and prioritisation discussions.

This experience reinforced for me that service design extends beyond the interface. The outputs were recommendations for further evaluation, not implemented solutions or evidence that the satisfaction target had been achieved.
