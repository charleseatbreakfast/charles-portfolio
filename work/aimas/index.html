AIMAS · Campaign orchestration platform

From fragmented campaign tools to one connected workspace.

AIMAS is a campaign orchestration platform for marketing teams managing audience setup, multi-step journeys and campaign performance.

I defined the product framework and end-to-end interface from the ground up, covering information architecture, journey-building workflows and the core UI system.

The challenge

Campaign work was fragmented across disconnected workflows.

Campaign setup, audience definition, journey logic, execution and performance lived in separate contexts, making it difficult to understand the campaign as one continuous workflow.

Existing workflow

Reviewing the existing workflow revealed three structural problems.

  1. 01

    Journeys were managed without enough campaign-level context.

    A flat journey list made it difficult to see how related work belonged together.

    Design response · Campaign as the container
  2. 02

    Complex logic became harder to follow across disconnected steps.

    Triggers, conditions, actions and waits needed to be understood as one journey.

    Design response · A visible journey canvas
  3. 03

    Performance information needed to stay connected to the campaign being managed.

    Campaign data was more useful when it remained part of the working context.

    Design response · Performance in campaign context

Core design decisions

01

Campaign as the container.

Campaign became the shared product object connecting audience, journeys, execution status and performance—not simply another page in the navigation.

Information architecture decision

A master-detail structure kept the campaign overview and its related journeys on the same screen, so users could compare and manage work without losing the parent context.

Product architecture

02

Build complex journeys as a visible system.

The Canvas translated abstract “if–then” logic into connected nodes that marketers could construct, scan and navigate as one workflow.

Interaction challenge 01

Keep large journeys readable.

Challenge
A journey could grow towards 50 nodes, making its structure harder to scan.
Design decision
Use consistent colour semantics to distinguish triggers, conditions, actions and waits.
Why it mattered
Users could recognise the journey structure before reading every node.

Interaction challenge 02

Move between detail and the whole journey.

Challenge
Long journey paths made it easy to lose orientation while editing individual nodes.
Design decision
Add a mini-map and zoom controls for switching between macro navigation and focused editing.
Why it mattered
Users could inspect a local branch without losing its position in the wider journey.

03

Keep performance visible in context.

Performance was not treated as a separate reporting destination. It remained attached to the campaign users were already managing, preserving continuity from planning through interpretation.

Reflection

The biggest shift was not a new dashboard or canvas—it was giving campaign work a shared product structure.

Designing AIMAS meant defining the relationships between campaign context, workflow logic and performance—not treating them as separate screens.
