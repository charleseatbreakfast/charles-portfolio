FORTRESS · Mobile product experience

Building a More Coherent FORTRESS App Experience

Across the wider app experience, I contributed to UX/UI, visual language and interaction. This case study focuses on how those contributions came together across key product moments.

Project
FORTRESS Mobile App
Role
Product Designer
Period
Scope
UX/UI · Visual System · Icon & Illustration · Interaction · Motion

Experience challenge

Key transitional moments were missing, static or unclear.

The opportunity wasn’t to add motion everywhere—it was to improve the moments where users enter, wait and transition.

Onboarding
No first-use guidance—users entered directly at Login.
Loading
Repeated motion without clearer system feedback.
Splash
A static brand entry.

Motion across the product

Motion appeared where the product needed guidance, feedback or expression.

Key experience decisions

Motion had a job before it had a style.

Orient

Onboarding

Context
The previous app had no onboarding and took first-time users directly to Login.
Motion decision
Introduce information progressively instead of presenting everything at once.
Product value
Give new users a clearer and more guided entry into the app.

Respond

Loading

Context
The previous loading experience used repetitive bouncing motion that added activity without clearer feedback.
Motion decision
Replace repetitive movement with calmer branded loading feedback.
Product value
Make waiting feel intentional while keeping system status visible.

Motion craft

From static states to final motion in After Effects.

I designed and produced the motion assets in After Effects, iterating the sequence and timing from static interface states to final animation.

Interaction & visual consistency

Building a consistent visual language.

Alongside the interface work, I contributed to a more recognisable visual language through category icons, hero illustrations and reusable visual patterns across the app.

Icon system

Designing consistency across 100+ categories.

I designed more than 100 category icons as a recognisable, repeatable family that remained legible within the constraints of the product interface.

Reflection

Motion became most valuable when it clarified state, not when it simply added personality.

Across visual design and interaction, consistency came from giving each element a clear role—not simply making every moment more expressive.
